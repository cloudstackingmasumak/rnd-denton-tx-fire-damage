Why Immediate Board-Up Protects Your Home

The window between fire and weather damage is measured in hours, not days. A Denton forecast of rain tonight means your fire-damaged home could accumulate inches of water before morning. That water migrates into walls, destroying insulation and creating mold. It soaks into hardwood floors, warping them permanently. It saturates drywall, requiring replacement. The cost of water damage often exceeds fire damage. An open roof during heavy rain can ruin contents in upstairs rooms that the fire never touched. Insects and rodents see an open, abandoned structure as an invitation to nest. Within 48 hours, pests can establish colonies in walls and crawlspaces. Securing your Denton property immediately after fire stops these cascading problems before they start. Board-up teams work through the night if necessary, arriving hours after firefighters leave. Temporary plywood barriers go up over broken windows and doors. Tarping secures roof openings. The goal is to make your house weathertight and inaccessible to intruders before dawn.
